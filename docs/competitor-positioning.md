# Where Surge actually differs — and where it doesn't

Researched 16 Sep 2026 from public secondary sources (linked at the end). **Terms
change and vary by deal.** Before any of this goes on the website, in an ad, or
in a proposal, re-check the competitor's own current terms page — comparative
advertising has to be accurate on the day it runs, and these companies have the
legal budget to care.

---

## The thing worth leading on

"You can edit your own menu" is not a differentiator. Toast, Square, Lightspeed,
Clover and TouchBistro all let an owner change a price without calling anyone.
Leading on it makes us look like we have not used a competitor's product.

What the category actually does to restaurants is **lock them in** — three
separate ways, all of which Surge genuinely does not do today:

**1. The contract.** Toast commonly runs two-year terms, with some promotional
deals at three, auto-renewing for a further year. Clover runs three-year terms.
Early termination on Toast is reported at roughly $150 per remaining month, or
the balance of the unpaid subscription — landing in the $5,000–$10,000 range
depending on how much term is left. Clover's is smaller, around $300–$500.

**2. The card processing.** Toast requires you to process on Toast, and reserves
the right to raise processing rates during the contract on 30 days' notice.
Lightspeed is reported to charge roughly $400/month if you process elsewhere.
Clover and SpotOn are the more flexible ones — Clover lets you choose a processor
at signup — so do not claim the whole category forbids it.

**3. The hardware.** Clover only runs on Clover-branded hardware. Toast sells its
own terminals. TouchBistro runs on iPads, so it is not a differentiator against
them specifically.

**Surge's position, stated honestly:** no contract, the merchant keeps the card
processing they already have, and it runs on a tablet rather than hardware we
sell. The reason the second one is true is that *we do not process cards yet* —
which is a gap, but it is a gap that happens to line up with the single most
common complaint about the incumbents. Say it as "your processing stays where it
is," never as "we have chosen not to lock you in," which would be flattering
ourselves.

## Feature-for-feature

Surge's pilot includes everything below at no cost. On the incumbents several of
these are paid modules, which is the second real story: the advertised headline
price is not what an independent actually pays.

| | Surge (pilot) | Typical incumbent |
|---|---|---|
| Register, floor plan, table service | included | included |
| Kitchen display | included | Toast ≈ $25/mo; Lightspeed KDS from ≈ $12/mo per screen |
| Online ordering | included | Toast ≈ $75/mo; Square includes it |
| Inventory, purchasing, recipes, waste | included | usually a higher tier |
| Reservations + waitlist | included | usually a higher tier |
| Staff scheduling, time clock, labour reporting | included | Toast payroll ≈ $90/mo + $9/employee |
| QR ordering, kiosk, digital menu board | included | typically extra |
| Multi-location | included | typically a higher tier |
| Reports + CSV export | included | included |
| **Card processing** | **not yet — you keep yours** | included, and usually mandatory |
| **Payment terminal** | **not yet** | sold by the vendor |

Reported real-world monthly software spend once an independent adds the modules
they need: **roughly $150–$500** on Toast, $0–$153 on Square, $69–$399 on
Lightspeed. Headline tiers start at $0–$69.

## What we have coming

Only two things, and both are already stated publicly on the site, so this is
the approved wording to reuse:

> Card processing and a dedicated payment terminal are in development. Neither is
> available from Surge today. Your existing processor stays separate during the
> POS pilot. We'll share supported markets, hardware and pricing when they're
> ready.

Do **not** promise that our processing will be optional, cheaper, or
non-exclusive when it launches. We have not decided that, and a promise made in
an ad now is a promise a merchant will hold us to later.

---

## Do not claim

- A price after the pilot. There isn't one.
- That every POS forces its own processing — Clover and SpotOn don't.
- That we're cheaper. We're currently free, which is a different claim, and it
  ends.
- Any named competitor's numbers without re-checking them that week.
- Anything about our own card processing beyond "in development."

## Safe ad framing

Keep the category claim unnamed on screen — "most restaurant POS systems lock
you in" is accurate and defensible without picking a fight. Save the named,
cited comparison for the sales conversation and the website, where the terms can
be footnoted and kept current.
